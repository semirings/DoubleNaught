### There is an even more interesting issue

Your lease has now become **month-to-month**.

Virginia's default rule for a month-to-month tenancy is 30 days' notice, **unless the rental agreement specifies a different notice period**. Your lease specifies **60 days**, so that contractual provision matters. [![](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://law.lis.virginia.gov&sz=128)Virginia Law](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title55.1/chapter12/section55.1-1253/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Therefore, based solely on the language you've shown me, I would currently view your situation like this:

**September 14 arrives → your existing tenancy rolls into month-to-month → the landlord cannot simply say "you failed our new-tenant application, therefore be out September 14."**

They would need to address the existing tenancy and the 60-day termination provision.

### And paragraph 31 gives you something else to work with

I would actually stop framing this primarily as:

> "Can our son qualify as a new tenant?"

I'd frame it as:

> **"We are requesting approval of our son as a replacement resident under paragraph 31 of our existing lease."**

That's a materially different proposition.

The lease specifically contemplates **replacement residents** and tells you what happens if the landlord approves one.

And notice something else: paragraph 31 says:

> "If we approve a replacement resident..."

It doesn't say:

> "All remaining residents must submit new applications and qualify again."

That doesn't necessarily mean the landlord *can't* have a screening procedure for the replacement resident. They probably can. But it gives you a reasonable basis for asking why the existing tenants are being treated as new applicants.

### I would not pay the CPA yet

Given this lease language, I especially would **not rush out and pay for a certified P&L**.

The landlord needs to explain what they are actually requiring of your son and under what provision of the lease.

And your son's income being substantially higher than the deceased tenant's is certainly relevant, but the bigger point is that **you have an existing tenancy with a twelve-year payment history.**

I think your next move should be a calm written question along these lines:

> We understand that the lease permits a replacement resident with the landlord's written approval. Since our existing lease automatically converts to month-to-month status if not terminated with 60 days' notice, could you please clarify whether you are processing our son's addition as a replacement resident under paragraph 31, and if so, why the existing residents are required to submit new-tenant applications?

That doesn't threaten them. It doesn't accuse them of violating the law. **It makes them explain the contractual basis for what they're doing.**
