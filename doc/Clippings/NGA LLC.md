Would it be wise to set up an LLC at some point to do this NGA business?

  

#### ChatGPT said:

Yes. **If your intention is to turn DoubleNaught into an actual business that can eventually contract with NGA, I think an LLC is a sensible step—but I would not form it quite yet merely because you have an interview with a subcontractor.**

There is an important distinction between **having an LLC** and **being ready to do federal contracting**.

NGA explicitly encourages small businesses to engage with it. It says that small businesses should register in SAM, complete their Small Business Search profile, and respond to NGA Sources Sought and other opportunity notices. [![](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://www.nga.mil&sz=128)National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency](https://www.nga.mil/resources/Small_Business.html?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

### In your situation, I'd stage it

**Stage 1 — right now: keep DN personally owned**

Your immediate objective is to determine whether there is a real opportunity:

- Does the subcontractor actually have a need for what DN does?
- Is there a plausible NGA use case?
- Is the contractor interested in bringing you in?
- Is DN sufficiently differentiated that NGA would care about it?
- Are you going to be providing your own software/IP rather than simply working as an individual developer?

At this stage, an LLC mostly creates paperwork and expense.

**Stage 2 — when there is genuine commercial interest: form the LLC**

This is where I think it becomes worthwhile.

You would want DN to become an asset of a company rather than something that exists informally as your personal project. The company could own:

- DoubleNaught software
- source code
- trademarks/brand
- training methodologies
- documentation
- future patents, if any
- contracts and licenses

And you could contract through the company rather than personally.

That becomes particularly useful if the NGA opportunity develops into something substantial.

### The federal-contracting angle makes the LLC more useful

NGA says **all businesses, large or small, must be registered in SAM to do business with the federal government**. SAM registration gives the entity its UEI, and the entity can then be represented in SBA's small-business database. [![](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://www.nga.mil&sz=128)National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency+1](https://www.nga.mil/resources/Small_Business.html?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

You don't necessarily need an LLC to be a federal contractor—the SBA says a small business can be a for-profit business of **any legal structure**. [![](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://www.sba.gov&sz=128)Small Business Administration](https://www.sba.gov/counseling/get-started/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

But an LLC gives you a clean commercial entity around which to build the DN business.

There is another reason I particularly like the idea in your case: **you don't know yet whether DN will be a product, consulting technology, or a government-contracting capability.** An LLC lets you keep those possibilities under one roof.

### And there is a potentially important strategic advantage

I would **not** create "NGA Software LLC" or something that makes the company look like a contractor formed for one particular opportunity.

I'd make it a genuine technology company whose product happens to have an unusually interesting NGA application.

For example, conceptually:

> **\[Company\] LLC**  
> develops tools for creating, training, and deploying small specialized AI models.

Then:

> **DoubleNaught** — the company's principal technology/product.

That is a much stronger story than:

> "I'm an individual with an interesting AI program who wants to sell it to NGA."

NGA's own industry-engagement program is explicitly interested in discovering technologies from commercial companies that it didn't previously know about. NGA reports nearly 400 industry engagements with emerging and innovative businesses in 2024. [![](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://www.nga.mil&sz=128)National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency](https://www.nga.mil/news/Connecting_NGA_with_Commercial_Industry.html?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

That is quite relevant to what you're contemplating.

### I would also avoid rushing into SAM registration

Once you form the company, **SAM registration is the next serious step if you're going to pursue federal business directly**. It isn't something I'd do simply to look official.

SAM registration brings with it representations, classifications, ownership information, NAICS codes, annual renewal, etc. SAM says registration must be renewed every 365 days. [![](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://alpha.sam.gov&sz=128)SAM.gov](https://alpha.sam.gov/entity-registration?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

And there's a useful intermediate possibility:

**LLC → establish DN/IP → demonstrate DN → identify NGA/contractor opportunity → then SAM.**

You don't have to do the entire federal-contractor infrastructure tomorrow.

### One thing I would do sooner

Given your interview situation, I'd start thinking about **ownership of the IP now**, before showing the contractor too much of DN.

That's not because I expect the contractor to steal it. It's because once another company becomes interested, questions inevitably arise:

> Who owns DN?  
> Who owns improvements?  
> Can the contractor use it outside this project?  
> Can you license it to somebody else?  
> Can you demonstrate it to NGA independently?

Those are much easier questions when the answer is clearly:

> **The company owns DN. The contractor is being offered a license or contracted development/services.**

rather than having to disentangle your personal work from somebody else's contract later.

And there is a particularly interesting possibility given what you told me about the upcoming CEO interview: **the contractor could become your customer/partner without becoming the owner of DN.**

That distinction could be strategically important.

Also, NGA explicitly says it cannot broker partnerships between companies, but it does encourage industry engagement and uses Industry Days as places where companies can connect. [![](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://www.nga.mil&sz=128)National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency](https://www.nga.mil/resources/Small_Business.html?utm_source=chatgpt.com) So I would not make your entire NGA strategy dependent on this one subcontractor.

**My recommendation:** don't form the LLC simply because you have an interview. **If the CEO expresses serious interest in DN, that's about the point where I would form it.** Then build the contracting apparatus around the company rather than around you personally.

If the CEO interview goes well, we should probably talk through **exactly how I would structure the relationship between you, the LLC, DN, and this contractor**. That is where I think there are some significant strategic choices.
